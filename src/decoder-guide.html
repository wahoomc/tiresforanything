How to read a tire size

There are only about six ways tire sizes get written. Once you've seen them, you can read the sidewall on almost anything: a car, a semi, a zero-turn, a forklift, the wheelbarrow behind the shed. Find the size in the biggest raised letters on the sidewall (on a press-on forklift tire it may be stamped on the steel band), then match it to one of these.

Common questions

Where is the tire size on the sidewall?

It's usually the biggest raised text after the brand name. On cars and trucks, the original size is also on the label inside the driver's door jamb. On press-on forklift tires, it may be stamped on the steel band.

What does the R in a tire size mean?

R means radial construction. A dash in the same spot usually means bias-ply. Nearly all car and truck tires are radial; many small, farm and industrial tires are bias-ply.

What's the difference between P, LT and ST tires?

P is a passenger tire, LT is a light truck tire built for towing and hauling, and ST is a special trailer tire for trailers only. Don't put ST tires on a vehicle or passenger tires on a trailer.

Can I use a different size than what's on there now?

Only if the wheel size matches exactly and the new size is approved for your vehicle or equipment. Load and speed ratings matter too. A tire shop can confirm what fits.

Why are bike tire sizes so confusing?

Several sizing systems overlap, so a 29 inch, a 700c and a 28 inch tire can share a wheel. The ETRTO number, like 28-622, is the one to trust: the second number is the wheel size in millimeters.

Does a decoded size mean the tire fits my machine?

No. The decoder explains what the numbers mean. Fit also depends on the wheel, load rating, speed rating and your manufacturer's specifications, so always confirm against what's on your equipment now.
