How to read a tire size, any tire, in 5 minutes

Cars, trucks, trailers, semis, mowers, ATVs, forklifts, motorcycles and bikes. There are only about six ways tire sizes get written. Here they all are on one page.

By Brad, former Class 8 tire tech, Army Reserve motor sergeant and private pilotUpdated November 11, 2026About 7 minutes

Here's the good news. There are only about six ways tire sizes get written, and once you've seen them, you can read the sidewall on almost anything. A car, a semi, a zero-turn, a forklift, the wheelbarrow behind the shed.

If you'd rather skip the reading, type the size into our size decoder and it'll break it down for you. But it's worth knowing yourself, if only so the guy at the counter can't sell you the wrong thing.

First, find the size

It's molded into the sidewall, usually in the biggest raised letters after the brand name. On a car it's easy to spot. On a mower or a hand truck it's often small and covered in dirt, so grab a rag. On a forklift press-on tire, it may be stamped on the steel band instead of the rubber.

Can't read it at all? Your owner's manual or the sticker inside the driver's door (on cars and trucks) lists the original size.

Car and SUV tires: the most common one

Example: P225/65R17 102H

  • P means passenger tire. No letter at all is the European version, and it's basically the same tire.
  • 225 is the width in millimeters, sidewall to sidewall.
  • 65 is the aspect ratio. The sidewall is 65% as tall as the tire is wide. Lower number, shorter sidewall, sportier look, rougher ride.
  • R means radial construction. Nearly every car tire made today is radial.
  • 17 is the wheel diameter in inches. Your tire and wheel have to match on this one, no exceptions.
  • 102 is the load index. It's a code, not pounds; 102 means 1,874 pounds per tire.
  • H is the speed rating. H is 130 mph. S is 112, T is 118, V is 149, W is 168.

Here's a quirk. Width is in millimeters and the wheel is in inches, in the same size. Nobody can explain that to my satisfaction. It just is.

Light truck tires: LT and the big off-road sizes

Example: LT265/70R17 121/118S, Load Range E

Same layout as a car tire, with LT up front. These are built tougher for towing and hauling. You'll see two load numbers. The first (121) is the rating when the tire runs alone. The second (118) is for when it runs as a pair on a dually. The load range letter (C, D, E, F) tells you how stout the tire is.

Big off-road tires often use a different format called flotation sizing: 35x12.50R17. That's 35 inches tall, 12.5 inches wide, on a 17-inch wheel. All inches, all straightforward. I wish every size worked like that.

Trailer tires: ST

Example: ST225/75R15

Read it exactly like a car tire. The ST means special trailer. They're built with stiffer sidewalls to keep a trailer from swaying, and they're rated for trailer use only. Don't put them on your truck, and don't put car tires on your trailer. Our ST vs. LT trailer tire guide explains why.

Semi and commercial truck tires

Examples: 11R22.5 and 295/75R22.5

The first is an older inch-style size: 11 inches wide, radial, 22.5-inch wheel. The second reads like a car size: 295 millimeters wide, 75 aspect ratio, radial, 22.5-inch wheel. Yes, truck wheels come in half sizes like 19.5, 22.5 and 24.5. That half inch matters, so don't round it off.

I changed a lot of these in my late teens and early twenties, in the shop and on the side of the road. Here's something most people won't believe: once you develop the technique, two tire spoons can be just as fast as a tire machine, sometimes faster. They're heavy, though. I'm glad I was doing it at 20 and not 49. I could still do it if I had to. I just wouldn't want to do it full time.

Small tires: three numbers or two

Three numbers, all in inches, like 18x8.50-8 (lawn tractor or golf cart) or 25x8-12 (ATV):

  1. 18 is how tall the tire is.
  2. 8.50 is how wide it is.
  3. 8 is the wheel size.

That dash before the last number usually means bias-ply construction instead of radial. If you see an R there (like 25x8R12), it's radial.

Two numbers, like 4.80/4.00-8 (wheelbarrow or trailer dolly) or 6.50-10 (forklift): the first number is the width and the last is the wheel size, both in inches. When you see two widths separated by a slash, the tire fits either listing. It's one tire, labeled two ways.

Forklift press-on tires

Example: 21x7x15. That's 21 inches tall, 7 inches wide, and fits a 15-inch steel band. Three numbers with x's between them and no dash is your clue that it's a press-on. Our forklift tire guide covers how they get installed.

Motorcycle tires

Examples: 180/55ZR17 (sport bike rear) and 130/90-16 (cruiser front). Metric motorcycle sizes read like car sizes: width in millimeters, aspect ratio, construction, wheel size. Again, the dash means bias-ply. Older bikes and some cruisers use alpha sizes like MT90-16 that need a conversion chart. The decoder handles those, and our motorcycle tire guide lists factory sizes by bike.

Bicycle tires

Examples: 29x2.4, 700x28C and 28-622. Bike sizes are a mess, honestly. A 29-inch, a 700c and a 28-inch tire can all fit the same wheel. The one number you can trust is the ETRTO size, like 28-622: 28 millimeters wide, on a wheel with a 622-millimeter bead seat. Look for it in small print on the sidewall. If the second number matches your old tire, it fits. More in our bike tire guide.

What about the other numbers on the sidewall?

You'll also see a DOT code that tells you when the tire was made (our DOT date code guide covers it), a maximum load and maximum air pressure, and sometimes ply ratings. The maximum pressure on the sidewall is not the pressure you should run on a car or truck. Use the number on your door sticker or in your owner's manual.

Common questions

Can I put a different size tire on my wheel?

Only if the wheel size matches exactly and the new size is approved for your vehicle. Your tire shop can tell you what fits.

What does the dash in a tire size mean?

It usually means bias-ply construction. An R means radial.

Is 700c the same as 29-inch?

On the wheel, yes. Both use the 622 mm bead seat. The tires are different widths and built for different bikes, though.

Where is the size on a forklift tire?

On the sidewall, or stamped on the steel band of a press-on tire.

What do the load index and speed rating mean?

The load index is a code for how much weight each tire carries (102 means 1,874 pounds). The speed rating letter is the top sustained speed, like H for 130 mph.
