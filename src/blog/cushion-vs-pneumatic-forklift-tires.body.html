Cushion vs. pneumatic forklift tires

The difference between cushion and pneumatic forklift tires, where each one works, air vs. solid pneumatic, and why you usually can't switch a truck from one to the other.

By Brad, former Class 8 tire tech, Army Reserve motor sergeant and private pilotUpdated November 13, 2026About 4 minutes

Forklifts come in two basic flavors, and the tires are the easiest way to tell them apart. Cushion tires are for inside. Pneumatic tires are for outside. Simple enough, but the details matter, because picking wrong costs you in tires, floors and downtime.

The quick answer
Cushion
Solid rubber pressed onto the wheel. Made for smooth, dry indoor concrete and tight aisles. No flats.
Pneumatic
Big tires on a regular rim. The truck sits higher and handles gravel, dirt and rough pavement.
Air or solid
Air-filled pneumatic rides softest but can go flat. Solid pneumatic never goes flat and lasts longer.
Switch?
Usually not from cushion to pneumatic. A pneumatic truck can usually switch between air and solid pneumatic.

Cushion tires: built for smooth floors

Cushion tires are the same thing as press-on tires (our press-on forklift tire guide covers them in detail). Solid rubber bonded to a steel band, pressed onto the wheel.

  • Small and low to the ground, so the truck is compact and turns tight. Great for narrow aisles.
  • Made for smooth, dry concrete.
  • No air, so no flats.
  • On gravel, broken asphalt or dirt, they ride rough and wear out fast, and the truck can bottom out.

Pneumatic tires: built for rough ground

Pneumatic tires look like big truck tires and mount on a regular rim. The truck sits higher, has more ground clearance and handles rough surfaces much better. Lumberyards, construction sites, farm supply stores and outdoor yards.

They come in two kinds:

  • Air-filled pneumatic. The softest ride on rough ground. The downside is flats, and a flat forklift tire means a truck parked until it's fixed.
  • Solid pneumatic. Solid rubber shaped to fit the same rim. No flats ever, and they last longer, but the ride is firmer. Lots of yards switch to these after one too many nails.

Can I switch from cushion to pneumatic?

Usually not. A cushion forklift and a pneumatic forklift are built differently: different wheels, hubs, axles, fenders and clearance. You can't just bolt pneumatic tires onto a cushion truck. If your work has moved outside, you need a pneumatic truck.

What you can usually do is switch a pneumatic truck between air-filled and solid pneumatic tires, since they fit the same kind of rim. Some trucks use multi-piece rims, and those need a trained tech (our tire vs. wheel vs. rim guide explains why).

Which one do you need?

  • All indoor, smooth floors, tight aisles: cushion.
  • Mostly outdoors on rough ground, and flats are rare: air pneumatic.
  • Outdoors with nails, screws, scrap or glass, and downtime costs money: solid pneumatic.
  • A mix of indoor and outdoor on decent pavement: talk to a dealer. A pneumatic truck with solid pneumatic tires is a common answer.

Common questions

Are cushion and press-on tires the same?

Yes. Same tire, two names.

Can a cushion forklift go outside?

On a smooth, dry parking lot for a short trip, yes. On gravel, grass or rough ground, no.

Do solid pneumatic tires fit the same rim as air tires?

Generally, yes. That's the whole point of them.

Which lasts longer, air or solid pneumatic?

Solid pneumatic tires usually last longer and never go flat, but cost more up front.
