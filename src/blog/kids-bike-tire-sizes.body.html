Kids' bike tire sizes, 12 to 24 inch, explained

Find the right replacement tire or tube for a kid's bike: sizes by age, the decimal vs. fraction trap that trips everybody up, and valve types.

The decimal vs. fraction trap

Bike tire sizes come in two styles, and they don't mix:

  • Decimal sizes, like 20x1.95 or 16x2.125. These are what almost all kids' bikes use.
  • Fraction sizes, like 20x1-3/8 or 16x1-3/8. These are mostly found on some older bikes, folding bikes and wheelchairs.

Here's the catch. A 20x1.95 and a 20x1-3/8 do not fit the same wheel, even though both say 20. The fraction-style wheel is actually bigger. So when you shop, match the style on your old tire, not just the first number.

The surest way is the small ETRTO number on the sidewall, like 50-406. The second number is the wheel size in millimeters. If it matches your old tire, it fits.

Width: does it matter?

The second number (1.75, 1.95, 2.125) is the width in inches. You can usually go a little wider or narrower than what's on there now, as long as it clears the frame and brakes. When in doubt, match what came on the bike.

Tubes and valves

Almost every kid's bike has an inner tube. Buy the tube to match the tire size, and check the valve:

  • Schrader is the same valve as on your car. Most kids' bikes have this one, and you can fill it at a gas station.
  • Presta is skinny with a little lock nut on top. Mostly on nicer road and mountain bikes.

If you're tired of fixing flats, thorn-resistant tubes or a little tire sealant are worth the couple extra dollars. Our leak stops guide covers the options.

Check the rim strip, too. That's the rubber or cloth band inside the rim that covers the spoke holes and spoke ends. On used bikes it's often cracked, slid out of place or missing, and then the tube rubs on a sharp edge and goes flat again, no matter how many new tubes you put in. If the same tube keeps getting a small hole on the inside (rim side), the rim strip is your culprit. They're cheap, so swap it whenever you're in there. Buy it by wheel size (12, 16, 20 or 24 inch) and width.

How much air?

The right pressure is printed on the side of the tire as a range, like 35 to 65 psi. Kids' bikes tend to ride best toward the middle. Too low and they're hard to pedal and pinch-flat easily. Too high and they're bouncy and slippery.

Quick shopping checklist

  • Read the size off the old tire (both numbers)
  • Decimal or fraction? Match the style
  • Check the ETRTO number if you can find it
  • Matching tube, with the right valve type, and a rim strip if the old one is worn
  • A pump that fits that valve

Common questions

What size bike tire for a 6-year-old?

Usually a 16 or 20 inch, depending on height.

Will a 20x1.95 tire fit a 20x2.125 wheel?

Yes. Both are decimal sizes on the same 406 wheel. Only the width is different.

Will a 20x1-3/8 tire fit a 20x1.95 wheel?

No. They're different wheel sizes.

Can I use a car tire pump?

Yes, if the bike has a Schrader valve, which most kids' bikes do. Watch the pressure, because bike tires fill fast.
