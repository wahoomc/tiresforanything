CDL pre-trip tire inspection: what to check and what examiners look for

The tire part of a CDL pre-trip, in plain English: tread depth (4/32 steer, 2/32 others), inflation, cuts, duals, valve stems and the rule on recaps for bus front tires.

By Brad, former Class 8 tire tech, Army Reserve motor sergeant and private pilotPublished December 18, 2026 at 3:34 p.m. ETAbout 4 minutes

I drove charter buses and the pre-trip never felt like busywork to me. You've got a coach full of people behind you. The tires are the only thing holding all of it to the road.

Whether you're studying for the CDL test or you just want a solid daily routine, here's the tire part of the walk-around, with the federal rules behind it.

The quick answer
Tread depth
At least 4/32 inch on steer tires and 2/32 inch on all others.
Condition
No cuts to the cords, no bulges, no tread or sidewall separation, no audible leaks.
Inflation
Check with a gauge, cold. Thumping is not a pressure check.
Buses
No recapped, retreaded or regrooved tires on the front wheels.

Tread depth

Federal rules (49 CFR 393.75) say front tires on a truck, tractor or bus need at least 4/32 inch of tread in a major groove. Every other tire needs at least 2/32 inch. Measure in a main groove, not on a wear bar. A tread depth gauge costs a few dollars and settles any argument.

Cuts, bulges and separations

Walk every tire and look at both the tread and the sidewall. You're out of service if you see:

  • Ply or belt material showing through the tread or sidewall
  • Tread or sidewall separation
  • A cut that goes deep enough to expose cords
  • A flat or a leak you can hear

A bulge in the sidewall is internal damage, even if it looks small. Our sidewall bulge guide explains why it can't be repaired.

Inflation

The rules say a tire can't run below the cold pressure required for the load it's carrying. In the test, say you'd check pressure with a gauge. Plenty of drivers thump tires with a bat or mallet. That'll find a flat. It won't tell you a tire is 20 psi low, and on duals, a low tire hides behind its partner.

Duals

  • The two tires shouldn't touch each other.
  • Nothing should be stuck between them (rocks and chunks of debris love to wedge in there).
  • Both should be the same size and type. Mismatched duals overload the bigger one.

Valve stems, wheels and lug nuts

While you're down there: check that valve stems aren't cracked or leaking and have caps, look for rust trails or shiny spots around lug nuts (signs of looseness), and look for cracked or bent rims.

The bus rule

If you drive a bus, there's one more. Recapped, retreaded or regrooved tires aren't allowed on the front wheels of a bus. Steer tires on a coach matter, and you don't gamble with them.

What to say on the test

Examiners want to hear you name what you're checking and what a defect looks like. Something like: "Checking tread depth, at least 4/32 on the steer tires and 2/32 on the drives. Checking for even wear, no cuts, bulges or exposed cords. Checking inflation with a gauge. Valve stems not cracked or leaking. Duals not touching, nothing between them." Your state's CDL manual has the exact list it uses, so study that version.

Common questions

What's the minimum tread depth for a CDL inspection?

4/32 inch on steer tires, 2/32 inch on all other tires.

Can I use recaps on a bus?

Not on the front wheels. They're allowed in other positions.

Can I thump tires instead of using a gauge?

It finds flats, not low tires. Use a gauge for pressure.

What if my duals are different sizes?

That's a defect. The bigger tire carries more load and can fail.
