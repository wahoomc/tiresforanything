Is a sidewall bulge dangerous? Bubbles, cuts and when to replace

A bubble on your tire's sidewall means the inside is damaged and it can't be repaired. How to tell a bulge from a normal dent, what causes it, and what to do now.

By Brad, former Class 8 tire tech, Army Reserve motor sergeant and private pilotPublished December 11, 2026 at 8:15 p.m. ETAbout 4 minutes

You're walking out to the truck and something catches your eye. A lump on the side of the tire, like a blister. It wasn't there last week. Most people's first question is "can I drive on it?" The honest answer is: only as far as the nearest place to put on your spare.

The quick answer
What it is
Air has gotten between damaged cords inside the tire.
Can it be fixed?
No. The sidewall flexes too much for any repair to hold.
Is it dangerous?
Yes. It can blow out without warning, especially at highway speed.
What to do
Put the spare on or drive slowly to a tire shop. Replace the tire.

What causes a bulge

Almost always an impact. A pothole, a curb, a chunk of debris. The hit pinches the tire against the rim and breaks some of the cords inside, even if the outside looks fine. Air works its way into that weak spot and pushes the rubber out into a bubble. Sometimes it shows up right away. Sometimes it takes days.

Low-profile tires on big wheels get it more often, because there's less sidewall to cushion the hit. Underinflated tires are at risk too, since a soft tire pinches more easily.

Bulge or normal dent?

Here's the good news for some of you. If what you're looking at is a shallow dip or ripple in the sidewall, not a bump sticking out, that's usually an indentation. Tire makers say it's a normal part of how the tire is built, where layers of cord overlap inside. It's harmless.

Why it can't be patched

Tire repairs are only allowed in the tread area. The sidewall flexes every time the tire rolls, and Michelin puts it simply: any repair there would get the same stress that caused the damage. A plug or patch on a bulge isn't a fix. It's a gamble.

What to do right now

  1. Don't take it on the highway. Heat and speed are what make a bulge let go.
  2. Put on the spare if you have one, or drive slowly to the nearest tire shop.
  3. Check the other tires for the same thing, especially on the same side if you hit something.
  4. Have the wheel looked at. A hit hard enough to bulge a tire can bend a rim too.

How to avoid them

Keep tires at the pressure on your door sticker (our cold weather pressure guide explains why they drop in winter), slow down for potholes, and don't hop curbs. If you're shopping for a replacement, our size guide and car tire page will help you match it.

Common questions

How long can I drive on a tire with a bulge?

Only as far as you have to, slowly, to get it changed. There's no safe mileage.

Can a bulge go away?

No. The damage inside is permanent.

Will insurance or a road hazard warranty cover it?

Many road hazard plans cover pothole damage. Regular manufacturer warranties usually don't.

Do I need to replace both tires?

Not always. On all-wheel-drive vehicles, tread depths may need to stay close, so ask your shop.
