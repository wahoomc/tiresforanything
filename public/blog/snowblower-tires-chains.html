Snowblower tires and chains: sizes, treads and when to upgrade

Find your snowblower tire size, pick the right tread, and decide if chains are worth it. Common sizes like 13x4.00-6, 15x5.00-6 and 4.80-8 explained.

By Brad, former Class 8 tire tech, Army Reserve motor sergeant and private pilotPublished December 3, 2026 at 11:38 a.m. ETAbout 3 minutes

A two-stage snowblower is basically a small tractor that only works when the weather is at its worst. And like a tractor, it's only as good as its grip. If yours spins and wanders on packed snow or the icy end of the driveway, the tires are usually the problem, not the machine.

Reading a snowblower tire size

Snowblower tires use the same small-tire sizing as mowers and wheelbarrows. 15x5.00-6 means 15 inches tall, 5 inches wide, on a 6-inch wheel. A two-number size like 4.80-8 means 4.8 inches wide on an 8-inch wheel. Our tire size guide walks through every format, and the size decoder will break yours down.

Can't read the sidewall? The owner's manual or the model sticker on the machine will list it.

Pick the right tread

  • Chevron or X-shaped snow treads. The common factory style. Fine for fresh, fluffy snow on a paved driveway.
  • Aggressive snow treads (sold under names like Snow Hog or Polar Trac). Deeper, sharper lugs that dig into packed snow. A real upgrade if the factory tires are worn.
  • Chains. The best grip on ice and packed snow, especially on sloped or gravel drives. They do chew up pavers and stamped concrete, so think about what you're clearing.

Buying chains that fit

Chains are sold by tire size, so buy them for the exact size on your sidewall. Put them on in the garage before the storm, not in the driveway at 6 a.m. with your fingers going numb. Snug them up after the first few minutes of use, since they settle in.

Keep the air right

Cold drops tire pressure (about 1 psi for every 10°F), and small tires don't have much to lose. A soft tire on one side makes the machine pull. Check them at the start of the season with a low-pressure gauge, and fill to what the manual or sidewall says. Our cold weather pressure guide explains why.

Flat every fall?

Snowblowers sit all summer, and the tires slowly leak down or crack. If yours is flat every time you drag it out, you've got options: a preventive sealant, a new tube, or flat-free tires that can't go flat at all. Our leak stops guide compares them.

Common questions

Do I need chains on my snowblower?

On a flat paved drive with fresh snow, usually not. On a slope, gravel, or packed snow and ice, chains make a big difference.

Can I put bigger tires on my snowblower?

Stick to the original size. Bigger tires can rub, change the auger height, and may not fit the axle.

Do snowblower tires need tubes?

Some do and some are tubeless. Check what's on there now; if it keeps leaking at the rim, a tube often fixes it.

Should I replace both tires?

Yes. Matching tires keep the machine tracking straight.
